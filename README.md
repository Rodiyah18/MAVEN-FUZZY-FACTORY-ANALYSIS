E-Commerce Sales Performance & Customer Behaviour Analysis

Project Overview

This project focuses on analysing the sales performance and customer behaviour of Maven Fuzzy Factory, an e-commerce business.

Using the Maven Fuzzy Factory dataset, I explored revenue performance, product sales, refunds, seasonal trends, and customer sessions across different devices to identify business opportunities and areas requiring improvement.

The analysis was carried out using Power BI, with SQL queries used to retrieve and investigate relevant information.

Project Objectives

- Evaluate overall sales and revenue performance.
- Analyse revenue, Cost of Goods Sold (COGS), and gross margin.
- Identify top-performing products and revenue concentration.
- Examine product refund patterns.
- Investigate monthly revenue trends and seasonal fluctuations.
- Analyse customer sessions across desktop and mobile devices.
- Generate actionable business insights and recommendations.

Dataset Understanding

The dataset was obtained from Maven Analytics.

Before starting the analysis, I consulted the accompanying data dictionary to understand the structure of the dataset, the meaning of each column, and the information represented by the different tables.

During my investigation, I discovered that the dataset had already been cleaned. This allowed me to focus on data modelling, analysis, and generating meaningful business insights.

Key Business Insights & Recommendations

1. Healthy Gross Margin, but High Revenue Concentration

- Total Revenue: $1.939M
- Total COGS: $722K
- Gross Margin: Approximately 63%
- The Original Mr. Fuzzy generated $1.21M, contributing approximately 62% of total revenue.

Recommendation: Protect the supply and quality of the best-selling product while developing strategies to increase sales of the other products through bundling and cross-selling.

2. Mr. Fuzzy Accounts for the Largest Share of Refunds

- Total Refunds: $85.3K
- Mr. Fuzzy Refunds: $61.8K
- Contribution to Total Refund Value: Approximately 72%

Recommendation: Investigate refund reasons, including product defects, sizing issues, shipping damage, and customer expectation gaps. Addressing the major causes could help reduce refund costs.

3. Birthday Sugar Panda Has the Highest Refund-to-Revenue Ratio

- Revenue: $230K
- Refunds: $13.8K
- Refund-to-Revenue Ratio: Approximately 6%

In comparison, Forever Love Bear recorded approximately 2%, while Hudson River Mini Bear recorded approximately 1%.

Recommendation: Review the product's quality, descriptions, and customer feedback to identify possible causes of the high refund ratio.

4. Revenue Shows Seasonal Fluctuations

Revenue peaked in February before declining to its lowest point in April. It then increased steadily from July through December.

Recommendation: Improve inventory planning and advertising ahead of the fourth quarter. Introduce targeted promotions and product campaigns during weaker sales periods to help improve revenue performance.

5. Desktop Dominates Website Sessions

- Desktop Sessions: 327K (69%)
- Mobile Sessions: 146K (31%)
- Overall Session-to-Order Conversion Rate: Approximately 6.8%

Recommendation: Investigate conversion rates by device to determine whether mobile users experience barriers during the purchasing process. Review mobile page speed, navigation, and checkout experience.

Key Takeaway

The analysis revealed that although Maven Fuzzy Factory maintains a healthy gross margin, its revenue is heavily dependent on one product. It also highlighted opportunities to improve refund management, product performance, seasonal sales planning, and customer experience.

Beyond the analytical findings, this project challenged me to step outside my comfort zone and learn the fundamentals of SQL to complete the task requirements.

It reinforced an important lesson: Data analytics goes beyond cleaning data and building attractive dashboards. It is about understanding the numbers, identifying patterns, and translating findings into meaningful business decisions.
